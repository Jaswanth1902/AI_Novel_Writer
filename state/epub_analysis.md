When Tejaswini received her assignment, she turned back to them with a small, reassuring smile. &#x201c;You&#x2019;ll do well,&#x201d; she said simply. Then she left for her site. Bennett followed soon after, his expression already set, focused. Chaitanya and Anil remained. They&#x2019;d been assigned to the same examination site, their numbers close Chaitanya only two behind Anil. Neither of them commented on it, but the proximity felt strangely grounding. Within minutes, they found themselves standing among a large crowd nearly a hundred students gathered together, all air benders like them. The atmosphere was tense but contained, filled with quiet murmurs and restless shifts of weight. A few evaluators stepped forward, their presence enough to still the noise. One of them raised a hand. &#x201c;Attention,&#x201d; the evaluator announced, voice carrying easily across the group. &#x201c;We&#x2019;ll begin by outlining the rules.&#x201d; The murmurs died at once. &#x201c;This examination is practical,&#x201d; they continued. &#x201c;There will be no written component. Each of you will be evaluated individually.&#x201d; A gesture to the side, where a marked platform and several stations were already being prepared. &#x201c;You will complete four phases in sequence. You may not advance if you fail a phase. The methods you use are your own but the objectives are fixed.&#x201d; A pause, deliberate. &#x201c;Phase One. Interception.&#x201d; Several assistants stepped forward, kunai already in hand. &#x201c;Three kunai will be thrown directly at you. You will remain in position and stop all three mid-air. Any movement from your mark, delayed response, or deflection past your body will be counted as a failure.&#x201d; A ripple of unease passed through the crowd. &#x201c;Phase Two. Directed Strike.&#x201d; One of the evaluators lifted a single kunai, letting it catch the light. &#x201c;You will raise the weapon using air and strike a distant target three times. Each strike must be intentional. Loss of control or misalignment will be noted.&#x201d; They moved on without waiting. &#x201c;Phase Three. Multi stream Control.&#x201d; Three light objects were brought forward and placed apart. &#x201c;You will maintain three separate air streams simultaneously left, right, and forward for ten seconds. All objects must remain aloft. Dropping even one ends the phase.&#x201d; Some students shifted now, tension tightening. &#x201c;And finally Phase Four. Sustained Defense.&#x201d; At the far end, a tree stump was dragged into place. A chalk circle was drawn around it. A balloon was set gently on top. &#x201c;You will keep the balloon afloat above the stump, within the chalk boundary , for ten seconds,&#x201d; the evaluator said. &#x201c;During that time, kunai will be thrown at you.&#x201d; A brief pause. &#x201c;You may not leave the circle.&#x201d; Silence gripped the group. &#x201c;If the balloon touches the stump, exits the boundary, or you are struck the phase ends.&#x201d; &#x201c;Your number determines your order. Prepare accordingly.&#x201d; With that, the examination began. One by one, students were called forward. Some froze under pressure, their air faltering at the first phase. Others managed to advance only to fail later, misjudging timing or losing focus. A few were struck by flying kunai, forced to withdraw with blood on their sleeves and shaken expressions. And then there were those who passed moving through the phases with clean, practiced control. The line thinned steadily. By the time Anil&#x2019;s number was called, the weight of the test was unmistakable.

# Chapter 25: The Orphanage of Ash

The Langford Municipal Orphanage sat backed against the western palisade, a single-story redoubt of fieldstone and rough lime mortar.

Inside, the low ceiling of adze-hewn pine beams trapped the thick, dry heat of a central brick stove fueled by coal briquettes. Thirty children, wrapped in rough grey woolens and patched sheepskin vests, sat on woven rush mats across the floor. They did not run or shout. They sat with their knees drawn to their chests, carving wooden whistles or watching the doorway with wide, unblinking eyes that had witnessed the collapse of mining shafts and the sudden arrival of frontier casualty carts.

Matron Sarah stood by the bread oven, wiping flour from her cracked, calloused forearms with a rough linen cloth. Her hair was iron-grey, bound tightly behind her neck with a scrap of leather thong.

"They scratch when the coal fire dies down," Sarah said, her voice dry and steady, though her fingers trembled as she retied her apron string. "Past the second watch of night. Down in the root cellar where we keep the salt pork and the winter turnip bins. It sounds like nails dragging across wet slate."

Lyra unhooked a tin lantern from her belt, striking a spark on a sulfur-tipped cedar match. The wick caught, casting yellow tallow glare across the heavy oak trapdoor set into the corner floorboards.

"Stay above with the children, Matron," Lyra said. "Bar the door behind us. If you hear wood shatter, take them through the rear wicket gate into the timber yard."

Tejaswini took post at the head of the cellar stairs, her drawn sabre resting flat against her thigh, its steel edge catching the lantern light.

Chaitanya led the descent down the six stone steps.

The cellar was cold, five feet high, and smelled of decaying turnip leaves, brine from open sauerkraut barrels, and damp cellar clay. Condensation ran down the fieldstone foundation walls in glistening beads, dripping into the gravel floor with hollow, rhythmic clicks.

Chaitanya knelt in the center of the floor.

He laid his lantern aside, pressed both palms flat against the limestone flagstones, and laid his left cheek directly against the cold rock.

His breathing slowed, matching the ambient temperature of the room. Through the dense crystalline lattice of the stone, his nervous system registered the acoustic reflections. The stone beneath the western wall was dense, bonded to bedrock. But beneath the center drain, twelve paces from the food bins, the stone did not reflect mass.

It hummed.

A hollow air pocket, seven feet wide, ran beneath the mortar joints: a conduit recently excavated through the packed river gravel.

A sickening, sweet stench of rotting marrow seeped through the cracks between the floor slabs, thick enough to coat the tongue with the taste of sulfur and spoiled tallow.

"It is directly beneath us," Chaitanya whispered.

The mortar between the center flagstones gave way with the sound of snapping bone.

The flagstones buckled upward in an explosion of grey loam and shattered stone. From the dark void below, a shape lunged into the cellar glare.

It was a Tier Two Vetala.

Its frame was that of an emaciated human stretched seven feet in length, its joints inverted like the hind legs of a hunting hound. Its flesh was grey, leathery, and slick with the same black bile seen at the mill, hanging loose over ribs that flared open like iron teeth. Its face was a distorted parody of humanity: no nose, lips rotted away to expose two rows of jagged brown needles, and empty orbital sockets burning with faint, phosphorescent green miasma.

The creature’s jaw split sideways into three hinged sections.

From its throat came a sound that froze the marrow in the bones: the high, quavering wail of a three-year-old child crying out for its mother in the dark.

Lyra’s breath hitched violently. Her hand froze halfway to her skinning knife, her eyes widening with a sudden, suffocating horror as the acoustic mimicry struck the auditory centers of her brain.

The Vetala lunged forward, five-inch bone talons aimed directly for Lyra’s throat.

Chaitanya moved without hesitation.

He did not attempt to counter the creature with brute force. His left hand whipped upward, releasing a high-frequency acoustic pulse through the air between the monster’s jaws. The compressed sound wave collided with the resonant frequency of the mimic cry, shattering the sonic wave like a dropped pane of glass.

The hypnotic paralysis broke. Lyra stumbled backward, gasping as her boot hit the turnip barrel.

In the same split-second, Tejaswini drove her sabre downward through the floor opening from the stairs, channeling a jet of orange flame into the subterranean tunnel. The fire roared down the pipe, burning away the oxygen and incinerating two smaller scavengers attempting to follow the alpha through the breach.

Chaitanya closed the distance.

His left foot planted into the shattered flagstone, his right hand shooting forward like an iron hook to grasp the Vetala’s elongated lower jaw. His left palm slammed into the base of its skull.

*Rotational pressure shear.*

With a brutal, simultaneous twist of his wrists and an internal burst of displaced atmospheric pressure, Chaitanya broke the creature's cervical column.

A sound like green timber snapping echoed through the low cellar.

The Vetala’s limbs went rigid, its jaw hanging loose, the green phosphorescence in its eye sockets sputtering out into dark smoke.

Chaitanya drove the creature’s skull down onto the flagstones. From his kit-bag he drew a four-inch forged iron spike coated in yellow sulfur grease. He positioned the iron point over the creature's third cervical vertebra and slammed the heel of his boot onto the head of the spike.

The iron drove two inches into the limestone floor, pinning the corpse's spinal core through bone and rock.

The leathery hide shuddered once, black marrow foaming from its nostrils, and lay dead.

Chaitanya stood up slowly, his breathing steady, his grey tunic unmarked save for a smear of cellar clay on his knee. He retrieved the lantern and held it over the jagged hole in the floor.

The excavation did not stop beneath the orphanage.

The tunnel stretched away to the west, four feet wide, shored up with stolen mining timbers, running straight through the municipal drainage conduit toward the high palisade and the dark Oakhaven Ridge beyond.

Lyra knelt beside the dead Vetala, her skin pale as chalk, her hands trembling as she wiped cold sweat from her brow. She looked into the tunnel, then up at Chaitanya.

"They didn't dig this to feed on thirty orphans," she said, her voice shaking with cold fury and dread. "They dug this to bypass the palisade walls."

"Yes," Chaitanya said, staring into the black mouth of the tunnel. "The vanguard has completed its survey. When the sun sets behind the ridge, the main horde will follow the drainage pipes into the center of the town."

He turned toward the stairs where Tejaswini stood with her smoking blade.

"Sound the town alarms, Tejaswini," Chaitanya commanded quietly. "Tell Bennett to open the northern evacuation gate and light the sulfur trenches. Langford has two hours before the breach begins."

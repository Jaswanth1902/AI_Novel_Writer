# Chapter 28: The Fall of the Danava

The Danava hit Bennett’s ice bulwark at thirty miles an hour.

The impact sounded like a bronze bell struck by an iron boulder. Twelve tons of charging muscle and armored chitin did not bounce; the seven-foot wall of reinforced ice and river granite disintegrated into a hail of jagged shrapnel that tore through the timber storefronts for thirty paces.

Bennett’s partisan snapped at the iron socket. The blunt kinetic force transferred through the ash haft into his gauntlets, throwing the northern cadet twenty yards across the street. His body crashed into the wreckage of an overturned grain cart, his armor dented flat against his ribs, coughing up dark mouthfuls of blood as his vision went black.

From above, Anil dropped from the slate eaves, screaming as he unleashed a double-handed hurricane gust aimed at the beast’s crimson eye slit.

The Danava did not even slow. Its massive left forelimb whipped around in a brutal, careless backhand.

The five-inch bone spade clipped the corner of the stone storehouse. Masonry exploded. The entire timber overhang collapsed in a roaring avalanche of oak beams, mortar, and slate shingles that buried Anil to his waist, pinning his legs under two hundred pounds of dead weight.

"Tejaswini!" Anil’s scream tore through the dust.

The Danava wheeled toward the center of the avenue, its crimson ocular slit locking onto Tejaswini.

She stood alone in the mud, ten paces from the beast. Her face was chalk-white, her uniform soaked in rain and soot, her breathing reduced to shallow, agonizing gasps. Every reserve of her fire core was burned down to ash, yet her grip on her cavalry sabre never wavered.

She reversed her stance, driving her back heel deep into the cobblestones. With a ragged, desperate cry, she channeled the final ember of her soul through the steel.

The blade ignited, turning from dull iron to glowing cherry, then to blinding white incandescence that hissed in the freezing drizzle.

She lunged forward under the monster's reaching claw.

The incandescent edge struck the Danava’s right knee joint, biting through the softer yellow connective tissue between the chitin plates. A fountain of boiling black bile erupted from the wound, searing Tejaswini’s leather sleeve to the skin.

The monster roared: a sound that shattered the remaining windows along the street.

It did not fall. It shifted its twelve-ton bulk onto its uninjured leg and reared its massive torso into the air. Its left forelimb, tipped with that terrifying spade of solid bone, rose ten feet above Tejaswini’s head, cocked like a guillotine to drive her into the mud.

Tejaswini looked up. Her sabre was wedged in the knee; her knees buckled under the sheer atmospheric downdraft of the falling limb. There was no time to pull the blade. No room to roll.

Chaitanya stepped between them.

He did not push her aside. He did not draw a weapon. He took the plumb-line of the falling spade, his boots sliding into the blood-soaked slush, his eyes black and devoid of light.

*The Fourth Inscription is a cage.*

*Matter is not substance. Motion is not element.*

*Nir-Hetu.*

Chaitanya did not breathe. He did not recite a seal. He reached into the seventy paces of atmosphere enclosing the avenue and seized the kinetic equilibrium of the air molecules.

With an inward, tearing contraction of his consciousness, he stripped every erg of thermal friction from the surrounding space. The air temperature across the street plummeted to absolute zero in a fraction of a millisecond; puddles of river water froze into sheets of white glass; raindrops turned to frozen hail that clattered on the stones.

All of that stolen thermal energy, thousands of degrees of raw kinetic fury, was condensed into a single microscopic point between his palms.

A needle of white-hot plasma, smaller than a sewing bodkin, humming at the frequency of solar flare.

At the exact same instant, Chaitanya created an inverse vacuum vortex directly inside the Danava’s dorsal respiration spiracles, evacuating every atom of barometric pressure from its internal thoracic cavity.

*Thermal detonation. Internal implosion.*

Chaitanya stepped into the shadow of the falling bone spade and drove his open right palm directly against the center of the monster’s armored breastplate.

The plasma needle penetrated the three-inch chitin like paper, followed instantly by the atmospheric void.

The laws of physics did not allow the beast to survive.

The extreme thermal shock of six thousand degrees colliding with the freezing, vacuum-sealed interior caused the Danava’s impenetrable breastplate to shatter from within. A concussive shockwave ripped through the monster’s thorax with the sound of a siege mortar detonating in a mine shaft. Black marrow, shattered chitin plates, and boiling vapor erupted outward in an apocalyptic cloud of gore and steam.

The spinal core at the base of the beast’s neck disintegrated into ash.

Yet in the final, dying reflex of its severed nervous system, the monster’s colossal left forelimb completed its downward arc.

The blow did not strike Tejaswini.

It struck Chaitanya across the left side of his torso with the weight of a falling oak tree.

The iron rivets on his candidate coat sheared off like straw. Three of his ribs caved inward; the clavicle shattered with a sound audible above the detonation.

The kinetic force hurled Chaitanya thirty feet across the street.

His body smashed through the heavy oak double doors of the grain storehouse, tearing through two interior timber partitions before collapsing into the darkness of the rear flour bins under an avalanche of splintered boards and white lime dust.

Outside on the street, the Danava fell.

Twelve tons of dead, hollowed chitin crashed onto the cobblestones. The impact shook the foundation of the town, sending cracks racing through the walls of the surrounding buildings before settling into an absolute, suffocating silence.

The monster did not move. The crimson slit was dark.

Rain continued to fall, washing the black bile into the drainage gutters.

From the wreckage of the timber dray, Tejaswini crawled forward on her hands and knees through the mud and gore. Her sabre lay forgotten behind her. Her hands were blistered, her hair wild, her voice cracking into a ragged, desperate sob as she reached the shattered doorway of the storehouse.

"Chaitanya!"

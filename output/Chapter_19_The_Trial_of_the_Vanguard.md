# Chapter 19: The Trial of the Vanguard

Rao’s war maul struck the blood-stone circle with the sound of a falling portcullis.

The limestone quarry floor shattered along a thirty-pace fracture. A concussive shockwave ripped through the loose scree, churning gravel into a spray of jagged shrapnel. Behind the wave, two slabs of bedrock reared up like the jaws of an open trap, hurtling toward Unit 07’s front line.

"Anchor!" Bennett roared.

The northern cadet slammed the butt of his iron partisan into the stone between his boots. Cold spread from his gauntlets in an instant frost-wave. He did not merely throw up an earth mound; he flooded the rising fissure with subterranean moisture, freezing it into a composite palisade of reinforced ice and packed granite three feet thick.

The bedrock collided with the frozen bulwark. The impact shook the quarry floor, sending cracks spidering up the ice face, but the barrier held. Chunks of shattered limestone tumbled harmlessly down the sides.

Before the dust settled, the air behind the wall turned to screaming blue fire.

Proctor Meera moved not as a runner, but as a discharge across a circuit. Her grey leather boots barely kissed the scree, carried forward by erratic, zigzagging bursts of electrostatic acceleration. In three strides she cleared Bennett’s frozen flank, her left copper baton thrust forward like an assassin’s stiletto.

A bolt of azure lightning, three fingers thick, snapped from the tip, aiming directly for Bennett’s exposed neck.

A shrill whistle cut through the roar.

From the top of the ice rampart, Anil dropped like a falcon. His hands swept downward in a violent double-palm strike, releasing an explosive downdraft that hammered the scree below. The sudden drop in air pressure dragged Meera’s forward foot half an inch to the side. Simultaneously, Anil loosed two brass throwing darts at her leading knee.

Meera twisted her torso in mid-stride with unnatural, serpent-like flexibility. Her right baton whipped around, deflecting the darts with a shower of copper sparks, but the lightning strike aimed at Bennett broke its arc, grounding harmlessly into a boulder behind him.

"Point clear!" Anil shouted, tumbling across the scree and springing back to his feet twenty paces away.

"Move!" Chaitanya’s voice cut through the chaos, quiet and unmistakable.

Tejaswini was already in motion. She drew her cavalry sabre in a single, fluid sweep that carried an arc of roaring orange flame across the quarry floor. She did not aim directly at Meera; she laid down an eight-foot curtain of superheated air between the lightning bender and the rest of the squad.

The intense heat expanded the atmosphere instantly, creating a turbulent thermal barrier that starved Meera of a stable ionized pathway.

Meera skidded to a halt on the gravel, her pale eyes narrowing through the shimmering heat distortion. She raised both batons, crossing them in an X-frame above her brow. The copper coils hummed at a pitch that set the teeth on edge. Static charge raised the hair along Chaitanya’s forearms; the scent of burning nitrogen filled the pit.

A twin-forked discharge tore through the thermal curtain, tracking the shortest path to ground: directly at Tejaswini’s heart.

Chaitanya stepped in front of her.

He did not raise an ice wall. He did not conjure a shield of wind.

His left foot slid forward in the loose gravel, locking his hips perpendicular to the incoming arc. His palms came together, separated by six inches of empty air, then snapped apart with a brutal, wrenching twist of his shoulders.

*Low-pressure vacuum corridor.*

The sudden evacuation of air molecules along the two-foot channel between his palms created a localized drop in dielectric resistance. The lightning, seeking the path of least impedance, was pulled off its forward trajectory into the artificial void.

The blue arc struck the empty air between Chaitanya’s hands with an ear-splitting crack.

Instead of allowing the charge to ground through his own flesh, Chaitanya twisted his right wrist downward toward the stone. The vacuum collapsed, dumping the concentrated electrical voltage directly into an iron survey spike driven into the quarry floor four feet to his right.

The iron spike detonated into a shower of molten slag. The current dispersed deep into the limestone bedrock, leaving the air smelling of fried stone and bitter copper.

Tejaswini did not pause to marvel. She sprang off the scree behind Chaitanya’s right shoulder, her sabre trailing a blazing crescent of white-hot heat that seared the fabric of Meera’s collar as the proctor leaped backward to clear the perimeter.

Across the arena, Proctor Rao’s roar shook the cliffs.

The earth-master had freed his maul. He advanced like a siege engine, kicking a three-hundred-pound limestone boulder out of the dirt with the toe of his basalt boot. The boulder spun end over end through the air, aimed directly at Anil, who was scrambling up a scree slope to reload.

"Bennett, hold the flank!" Chaitanya yelled.

Chaitanya pivoted on his heel, his grey tunic snapping against his ribs. He tracked the spinning mass of the boulder through the air, reading the angular momentum of its flight. With a sharp, upward whip of both arms, he formed a high-pressure wedge under the leading face of the rock.

The aerodynamic wedge did not halt the boulder’s momentum; it tilted the axis of its flight by four degrees.

The three-hundred-pound rock grazed past the edge of the scree embankment, missing Anil’s shoulder by six inches, and smashed into the cliff wall behind him in a violent explosion of powder and dust.

"My turn, cubs!" Rao was already upon them.

The veteran earth-master closed the distance with terrifying speed for a man of his bulk. He dropped his maul into the scree and lunged with his left arm, his massive gauntlet encased in three layers of jagged granite plates. The stone fist aimed straight for Chaitanya’s chest, carrying the kinetic force of a battering ram.

Chaitanya dropped his stance under the blow, the air rushing across his scalp as the stone fist passed inches above his hair.

From his blind side, Tejaswini arrived like an avenging fury.

Her boots dug into the gravel, driving all her momentum through her hips. She reversed her grip on the sabre, bringing the flat of the glowing blade up to intercept Rao’s wrist while her left palm slammed directly into the elbow joint of his stone armor.

A burst of blue flame exploded from her palm.

The sudden thermal shock of four hundred degrees against cold basalt caused the stone plates on Rao’s arm to fracture with a sound like pistol shots. Cracks webbed across the armor, shedding chunks of shattered rock into the dirt.

At the same instant, Bennett’s partisan arrived from the left, its iron point lodging precisely into the exposed leather seam beneath Rao’s shoulder guard, pinning the giant’s arm against his torso.

Behind them, Anil’s wind blast kicked a cloud of fine limestone grit directly into Meera’s eyes as she prepared a second discharge, forcing the lightning bender to break her stance and parry three consecutive throwing darts that embedded themselves in the timber barricade behind her head.

For three heartbeats, the quarry fell dead silent.

Chaitanya stood balanced beneath Rao’s chest, his fingers pressed lightly against the center of the earth-master’s breastplate: ready to release a point-blank pressure shear into his ribs.

Tejaswini held her glowing blade at Rao’s throat, the heat singeing the black bristles of his beard.

Bennett held the spear steady, muscles trembling with the strain of bracing against the veteran’s mass.

High on the rim of the quarry, the brass bell of the watchtower rang twelve times.

The evaluation had ended.
